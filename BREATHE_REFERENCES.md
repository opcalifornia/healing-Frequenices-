# Breathe: references, and what we can legally use

Written for partner review. Not legal advice: have a lawyer confirm the trademark points before launch.

## How we use other people's work
- **Techniques are methods, not copyrighted text.** Counting breaths, paces and ratios (box, 4-7-8, cyclic sighing, resonance at about six a minute, pursed-lip) are ideas anyone may teach. We write our own instructions and our own words.
- **We link to sources instead of copying them.** Every technique card cites the paper. The "Learn more" list links to free pages and describes them in our own words.
- **Public-domain and open sources are the safest to quote.** US government pages (NIH, NCCIH, NIGMS, FDA, CDC) are generally not copyrighted. NHS content is published under the UK Open Government Licence, which allows reuse with attribution. Open-access papers (PubMed Central) can be linked and summarized; quote only briefly with attribution unless the licence (often CC BY) allows more.
- **Do not copy:** podcast transcripts, course or book text, app scripts, guided-audio, diagrams or photos from other apps and sites, or Cleveland Clinic or Mayo text.

## Names to be careful with (check before launch)
- **BOLT / Oxygen Advantage** (Patrick McKeown): we use a generic breath-hold timer and credit him. Confirm whether "BOLT" is a registered trademark before using it as a product name.
- **4-7-8**: popularized by Dr. Andrew Weil. Confirm trademark status. We describe it as a pattern and cite a study.
- **Wim Hof Method**: a trademarked method. We do not use the name in the product; the "Power breathing" card says "inspired by a well-known style" and sits behind a safety screen.
- **Physiological sigh**: a plain-language name used by researchers and Dr. Huberman; the study calls it cyclic sighing. Safe to describe, cite the paper.
- **Calm, Headspace, Breathwrk, Othership, Open** and others are competitors' trademarks. Do not use them in our copy (we also avoid the word "calm" in Breathe).
- Do not imply endorsement by any person or organization we cite.

## What we built from the research
| Added | Why | Evidence we state |
|---|---|---|
| Resonance breathing (5 in, 5 out) | The best-studied slow pace | Strong for heart rate variability, moderate for mood and stress |
| Pace finder (five 1-minute paces, rated by feel) | Everyone's easiest slow pace differs | Moderate; by feel, not a measurement |
| Longer breath out (4 in, 8 out) | The exhale is the likely active part of cyclic sighing | Weak (ratio not tested directly) |
| Pursed-lip breathing | Taught in lung care for breathlessness | Moderate (clinical guidance) |
| Equal breathing, no holds | A gentle option when holding feels uncomfortable | Weak |
| Health screen before gated practices | Safety for hyperventilation and holds | n/a |
| "Stop and breathe normally" button, eyes-closed mode, "Again" button | Safety and ease of use seen across top apps | n/a |
| Learn more list and a safety list | Free, verified sources | See below |

## Left for later
- **Camera heart-rate pace finder.** True resonance finding uses heart rate variability readings. Phone-camera readings over a minute per pace are not reliable enough yet, so the pace finder is by feel. A wearable or chest-strap version is the right next step.
- **Separate voice and music volume**, forgiving streaks and one-tap restart on the lock screen.
- **Apple Watch haptics** and a widget (needs the native app).
- **Clinician review** of the safety copy, especially for pregnancy, epilepsy, asthma and cardiac conditions. Wording is "ask your clinician" because no authoritative source gave specific guidance.
- **Breath counting and diaphragmatic training** as guided lessons (little research found).
